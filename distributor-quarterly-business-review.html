How to Run a Distributor Quarterly Business Review

By Josh Rushlo · JBR Insights · Channel Strategy · November 25, 2026

A distributor quarterly business review (QBR) is a working meeting between a manufacturer and a distributor. Compare the last quarter with the targets you agreed on, identify where sales stalled, and decide what each team will do in the next 30 days. Leave with owners, due dates, and a check-in on the calendar.

What to Bring to the Review

Start with one distributor, a defined product line or territory, and the same review period in every report. Have the manufacturer’s channel lead and the distributor’s sales manager bring the numbers they own. Send the scorecard and prior commitments ahead of time so people can resolve obvious data gaps before the meeting.

  • Results against agreed targets. Bring net sales, new buying accounts, repeat orders, qualified opportunities, buyer meetings or demos, and on-time follow-through. Keep manufacturer invoices to the distributor and the distributor’s customer sales clearly labeled.
  • Evidence for the biggest gap. Depending on the issue, pull a few CRM notes, open or lost quotes, backorders, or repeat-order records. Ask the people who worked the accounts what happened.
  • Last quarter’s commitments. Bring the old action log. Check what was completed, what ran late, and whether the manufacturer delivered the support it promised.

If a number is missing, record who will verify it and by when. Carry a documented gap into the meeting instead of treating it as a zero.

A 45-Minute Distributor QBR Agenda

  1. 5 minutes for prior commitments. Review what closed, what’s late, and what needs a new owner or due date.
  2. 10 minutes for the scorecard. Compare actuals against targets and flag the result that most needs a decision. Ask what changed since the previous review.
  3. 15 minutes to work through the sales gap. Pick the most consequential gap and look at actual examples. Decide whether the issue is inventory, customer coverage, deal qualification, quote follow-up, or reorders. Use the distributor sales diagnostic when the cause is unclear.
  4. 10 minutes to agree on the next 30 days. Pick one priority and decide what the distributor will do and what the manufacturer will supply. Define what completed work looks like.
  5. 5 minutes to read back the commitments. Confirm a single accountable owner and due date for each action. Put a check-in on both calendars.

Completed Distributor QBR Example

Illustrative Midwest distributor, Q3 2026. In the worked scorecard, the distributor scored 80.4 out of 100. Net sales were $96,000 against $120,000, and 12 of 20 eligible buyers ordered again. Eight of 10 prior commitments were completed on time.

The distributor completed 12 meetings or demos against a target of 10, but opened only five qualified opportunities against a target of eight. I’d pull a few meeting notes and check for a named buyer, a documented need, and a dated next step. That tells the two teams whether they need to change whom the reps call, what happens on the call, or how they follow up. For this example, the teams agree to focus joint visits on five target accounts and provide product support tailored to those accounts.

Example Action Log

  • October 9, distributor sales manager. Assign five target accounts and book joint visits. Check that all five accounts have an assigned rep and the visits are on the calendar.
  • October 16, manufacturer channel lead. Send application and demo support. Check that the distributor receives it for all five accounts.
  • October 30, manufacturer channel lead. Bring both teams together to review newly qualified deals. Count deals that meet the qualification criteria and give each stalled next step an owner and date.

What Happens After the QBR

Send the decisions and action log to both teams after the meeting. At the 30-day check-in, open the same log and ask what the work produced. If calls happened and deals still didn’t advance, review those calls and change the next action. Bring the results and any open commitments to the next quarterly review.
