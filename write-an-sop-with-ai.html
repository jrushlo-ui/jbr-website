How to Turn an Employee Walkthrough Into a Useful SOP With AI

By Josh Rushlo · JBR Insights · Everyday AI for Small Business · November 27, 2026

Ask the office coordinator how a return gets from the front counter to receiving. Then ask the person who covers lunch. If you get two answers, you’ve found a good use for AI. Have the coordinator talk through an ordinary return while someone takes notes. Ask the AI to turn those notes into the first draft of a standard operating procedure (SOP) the lunch cover can actually follow.

A checklist can remind an experienced employee to log a return. Someone doing it for the first time needs to know which number to look up, what counts as a match, where to put the box, and who decides what to do when the number is missing. Those details come from the people who handle returns.

From a counter employee’s walkthrough to a usable first draft

Here’s a fictional office coordinator explaining how the counter accepts boxed returns.

First I ask for the return number. I look it up in the returns log and match the item and quantity to what’s on the approval. If it all matches, I enter the drop-off date, write the number on a tag, and put the tagged box on shelf R. Receiving picks it up there. If there’s no number or something doesn’t match, I keep the box at the counter with the customer and call the office lead before accepting anything. I never promise a refund at the counter.

Give the AI the walkthrough and the following instruction.

Write a short procedure for the person covering the counter. Include when it applies, who does the work, how they know they’re finished, and when they need the office lead. Use only the steps in the walkthrough. List unanswered questions separately for the coordinator.

What the person covering lunch should see

Accepting a boxed return at the front counter. The counter employee handles the drop-off. The office lead handles exceptions.

  1. Ask for the return number and find the approval in the returns log.
  2. Compare the item and quantity at the counter with the approval. If there’s no matching approval or either detail differs, keep the box at the counter with the customer and call the office lead before accepting it.
  3. For a match, enter the drop-off date in the log. Write the return number on a tag, attach it to the box, and place the box on shelf R for receiving.

The counter employee’s part is complete when the dated log entry and the tagged box on shelf R carry the same return number. The customer can be told the box has been received. Refund decisions fall outside the counter employee’s authority.

Test it with the person who will use it

Hand the lunch cover a practice box and a made-up approval. Let them run one matching return and one with an extra item while the coordinator watches quietly. If they stop because there are two returns logs, add the location of the right one. If the office lead is out, the coordinator needs to establish who covers exceptions before adding that instruction. Put the coordinator’s name on the approved copy so employees know where corrections go.
