Fractional Sales Leadership for $5M–$25M Distributors

By Josh Rushlo · JBR Insights · Fractional Leadership · November 25, 2026

Fractional sales leadership makes sense when a distributor has a sales team in place and needs someone to manage it regularly on a part-time schedule. For a business with $5M–$25M in annual sales, that might mean taking pipeline reviews, account priorities, and rep coaching off the owner’s plate. The workload and the authority you can delegate tell you whether the arrangement will work.

When Fractional Sales Leadership Makes Sense

Look at what keeps coming back to you as the owner. If you run every sales meeting, chase updates on open quotes, and settle territory questions, the team needs someone to own those decisions and follow through between meetings.

  • You have people to lead. Reps or account managers already sell and serve customers. They need clearer priorities, coaching, and a manager who can hold them to commitments.
  • You can name the work. Start with a defined scope, such as running the weekly pipeline review, coaching the team, and tightening account ownership in one territory.
  • You can delegate decisions. The leader can see the relevant sales and margin data, set priorities within agreed limits, and ask people to follow through. The team knows who to go to between scheduled working days.

Consider full-time leadership if several branches need daily management, a large hiring plan is underway, or customer and personnel escalations routinely require the sales leader’s immediate involvement. If the gap is limited to one workflow, like quote follow-up, a focused project may be enough. For help with the hiring decision, read the fractional versus full-time sales leadership comparison.

Who Owns What

Agree on decision rights before the first sales meeting. Tell the team who can assign work, what requires approval, and who handles urgent issues when the fractional leader is away.

RoleWhat they own
Owner or general managerRevenue and margin priorities, budget, compensation and headcount decisions, and approval limits for pricing and territory changes.
Fractional sales leaderThe sales plan, weekly pipeline and forecast reviews, rep coaching, and account priorities. Resolves day-to-day sales ownership questions within the agreed authority and escalates exceptions.
Branch manager or designated backupDaily coverage and urgent decisions within their authority when the fractional leader is away. Escalates issues outside those limits.
Reps and account managersCustomer relationships, quote follow-up, accurate opportunity updates, and completing the next agreed action with each customer.
Pricing and operations leadsPricing approvals within assigned limits, stock and lead-time commitments, and fulfillment. The sales team checks with them before promising an exception.

If a quote is stalled over a discount, the rep owns the next customer conversation. The fractional leader coaches the approach and sets a follow-up deadline. The designated pricing approver decides whether to make the exception.

What the First 30 Days Look Like

Week 1 · Agree on the scope and look at the numbers

Meet with the owner and the people running sales, pricing, and operations. Agree on the problem to solve, the decision limits, and who covers urgent issues. Pull open quotes, recent orders, gross margin by account or product line, and the current territory list. Start with the reports the team uses today.

Week 2 · Find where work is getting stuck

Spend time with reps and inside sales. Review a sample of recent wins, losses, and open quotes. Look for work the sales leader can affect, such as unclear account ownership or quotes without a dated next step. Record a baseline and pick one or two priorities the team can act on this month.

Week 3 · Run the first sales reviews

Hold a short pipeline meeting and coach reps on active accounts. Start with a handful of deals that need a decision. Each one leaves the meeting with a clear owner, a next customer action, and a date. Bring a specific inventory, pricing, or service issue to the person who can resolve it.

Week 4 · Review what changed and set the next priorities

Check whether reps are following through and whether the owner is getting fewer routine escalations. Use the same measures you used for the baseline. For example, if nine of 20 open quotes had a dated next step at the start, go back through those 20. Record which progressed, which closed, and which still need action. Use those results to decide what to keep working on over the next 60 days.

By day 30, you should have a written division of responsibilities, a sales meeting the team can keep running, and a short list of priorities with owners and dates. Booked revenue may take longer to move. You can already see whether the team is handling customer work more consistently and whether the part-time schedule covers the decisions it needs to.
