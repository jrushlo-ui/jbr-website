Fractional Sales Leadership for $5M–$25M Distributors

By Josh Rushlo · JBR Insights · Fractional Leadership · November 25, 2026

A rep tells you a big quote is “still out there.” You ask when they last spoke with the customer and what’s supposed to happen next. They open their email to check. You’ve had some version of that conversation three times this week.

If you run a distributor doing $5M–$25M a year and every conversation about a stalled sale starts with you, it may be time for somebody else to manage that work.

When a Fractional Sales Leader Makes Sense

A fractional sales leader manages an existing sales team on a part-time basis. It’s a good fit when your reps know their products and customers, but the work of deciding which accounts to grow, coaching the reps, and following up on commitments keeps landing on your desk. Start by looking at how often those decisions come up during an ordinary week.

Think about the calls you keep getting. If it’s usually a rep needing help moving a deal forward or deciding where to spend time next week, you can hand that work to a sales leader and agree on who covers urgent decisions between their working days. Several branches that need daily supervision or a team going through a stretch of heavy hiring may need a dedicated leader. The fractional versus full-time comparison walks through that decision.

What You Hand Over

You set the growth and margin goals, approve budgets and hiring, and decide how much authority to delegate. The fractional leader runs sales reviews, coaches reps on real accounts, and makes sure the team follows through. Your reps keep their customer relationships and make the calls they’ve committed to.

Take a quote that’s stalled over price. The fractional leader helps the rep figure out what the buyer actually needs. The rep goes back to the customer. If the deal needs a pricing exception, whoever you’ve given that authority makes the call.

What You Should Notice in the First Month

Start with the work that already takes up your time. Show the new leader a deal that should have closed, an account you want to grow, or the sales meeting you keep having to run. You should see them sit down with your reps, work through those accounts, and bring you a clear picture of what’s moving and where the team needs a decision from you.

By the end of the month, you should be able to walk into a sales review and see which deals need attention, who owns the next move, and when they’ll make it. Your reps should know what to spend their time on next. Revenue can take longer to show up. Look first for the rep who comes back with a date for the next customer call and follows through on it.
