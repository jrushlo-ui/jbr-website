Practical Ways to Use AI in a Wholesale & Distribution Sales Organization

By Josh Rushlo · JBR Insights · AI & Execution

AI is most useful in a sales organization when it makes good commercial work easier to do. The goal is not to bolt an AI tool onto every process. It is to find repetitive work, information bottlenecks, and preparation tasks where technology can give salespeople and managers more time for customers, coaching, and decisions.

Start With Friction, Not the Tool

Ask where the team loses time. Account research? Preparing for customer meetings? Summarizing notes? Writing routine follow-up? Pulling information from several systems? Building account plans? Turning sales activity into a useful management update? Those are better starting points than asking which AI platform the company should buy.

Improve Account and Meeting Preparation

AI can help organize public company information, summarize existing account notes, identify questions to investigate, and turn scattered information into a meeting brief. The salesperson still decides what matters. The value is reducing the time spent assembling the first draft of the picture.

Make Follow-Up Faster Without Making It Generic

Meeting notes can become action lists, follow-up drafts, CRM summaries, and internal handoff notes. But customer communication should still sound like the person who owns the relationship. AI-generated language that is not reviewed is an easy way to make a good account manager sound like everyone else.

Help Managers See Patterns

When the underlying data is reliable, AI can help summarize pipeline changes, group common reasons opportunities stall, surface recurring customer questions, and prepare territory or account-review material. It should support management judgment, not replace it.

Use AI to Improve Internal Knowledge

Distribution businesses often have valuable knowledge scattered across product documents, pricing guidance, service procedures, emails, and experienced employees. A well-designed internal knowledge workflow can make approved information easier to find and reduce repeated questions. Access controls and data quality matter; faster access to bad information is still bad information.

Put Guardrails Around the Work

Before employees paste customer, pricing, financial, or proprietary information into an AI system, the company should understand what data is appropriate to use, which tools are approved, and where human review is required. Start with lower-risk workflows, establish expectations, and expand when the process is working.

AI should remove friction from the sales process—not remove judgment from the salesperson.
