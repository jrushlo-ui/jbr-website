Distributor Performance Scorecard

By Josh Rushlo · JBR Insights · Channel Strategy · November 25, 2026

A distributor performance scorecard tells you whether a partner is growing sales, winning new buyers, bringing customers back, and doing the work that leads to the next order. Set the targets together, score the same six measures each quarter, and leave the review with named owners and due dates.

The workbook includes a blank template, a completed example, and an action plan. You can also read the filled-out example below.

What to Measure on a Distributor Scorecard

A big purchase order tells you what the distributor bought. The next questions are who bought from the distributor, who came back, and what the reps are working on now. Use both the manufacturer’s invoice data and the distributor’s account activity to answer those questions.

  1. Net sales (25%). Use manufacturer invoices to the distributor less returns and credits. Use the same basis for the target and actual. Ask about initial stock orders or unusually large purchases when they explain a change.
  2. New buying accounts (15%). Count unique end customers who placed their first order for your line during the period. Exclude stock transfers.
  3. Repeat order rate (15%). Of eligible customers who purchased in the prior period, what share bought again this period? Agree on the cohort up front. Leave the rate blank when there are no eligible buyers.
  4. Qualified opportunities (15%). Count new deals opened in the period with a named buyer, a documented need, and a dated next step. Count each deal once.
  5. Customer-facing sales activity (10%). Count completed buyer meetings or demos linked to a named account and dated notes.
  6. On-time follow-through (20%). Divide commitments completed by their due date by all commitments due that period, including overdue items. Track which team owned each commitment so you can talk about what both sides need to fix.

How to Calculate the Score

Agree on targets before the quarter starts. Consider the territory, the maturity of your line, and the account base. For each measure, divide actual by target and multiply by the weight in points. A result of six new accounts against a target of eight earns 11.25 of 15 points. Cap each measure at its weight and floor it at zero. That keeps a burst of activity in one area from covering a miss elsewhere.

Enter zero when you measured zero. Leave an unknown result blank and agree on who will get the data. The workbook needs six scorable rows before it produces an overall score. A positive target is required for every row.

  • 85 and up. Maintain the plan or invest in the areas producing results.
  • 70 to under 85. Pick the most important gap and agree on a 30-day improvement plan.
  • Below 70. Review partner fit, manufacturer support, and both sides’ commitments before resetting the plan.

These are starting points for a review. Use the score to decide what to ask about, especially when stock availability, seasonality, or missed commitments on your side affected the result.

Twelve of 20 eligible customers ordered again. The teams completed eight of 10 commitments on time. Before asking for more meetings, I’d look at the 12 they already held. Were those calls with buyers who had a real need? Did anybody agree on a next step? Did the manufacturer get the distributor the product support it promised?

What Both Teams Do in the Next 30 Days

  • Distributor, by October 9. Assign five target accounts and book joint visits.
  • Manufacturer, by October 16. Send product applications and demo support for those accounts.
  • Both teams, by October 30. Review newly qualified deals and assign a person and date to every stalled next step.

At the next review, start with those commitments. If deals are getting stuck because a direct rep and a distributor are pursuing the same account, use the deal registration template to decide who leads the opportunity and who quotes it.
