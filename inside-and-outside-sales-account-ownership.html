How Inside and Outside Sales Reps Should Share an Account

By Josh Rushlo · JBR Insights · Sales Leadership · November 25, 2026

If a buyer emails your inside rep for a price and gets a second quote from the outside rep an hour later, they’ve just learned something about your company. Next time they may ask both people and see which answer they like better.

An inside and an outside rep can serve the same account well. Name one account lead, then give each person authority to finish the work the buyer brings them. The account lead looks after the whole relationship and settles questions about who takes the next step. Routine orders should be able to move while that person is out visiting customers.

Who handles the Tuesday morning reorder?

For an established distribution account, I’d usually give the inside rep repeat quotes, availability checks, order entry and updates on open orders. They know the order history, and the buyer already calls them. Give them access to the account’s current pricing, approved items and delivery instructions so they can answer in one exchange.

Give the outside rep work that benefits from a visit or a broader customer conversation. A second location is opening. A supervisor wants to change how a product is used. Purchasing wants one agreement across three plants. Any of those is a reason to bring the outside rep in. For an account that buys almost entirely by phone, the inside rep may be the right account lead. Pick the person who can keep track of the customer’s priorities.

One email with two different requests

In this fictional example, a purchasing manager emails the inside rep for another 24 cartons of shop towels. At the bottom she adds, “We’re setting up a second maintenance shop. Could somebody come out before we decide what to stock?”

The inside rep books the reorder on the existing terms, checks that Dana can call Thursday, and replies, “I’ve entered the 24 cartons. I’ve copied Dana, who covers your account in the field. She’ll call you Thursday morning to arrange a visit to the new shop.”

Dana owns the visit and the quote for the new shop. She adds the buyer’s request, the site contact and the callback date to the shared account record. The inside rep keeps the original order moving. When the new shop approves its stocking list, Dana hands the approved items and prices back to inside sales for reorders. The buyer knows whom to call the next time they run low.

Give the manager one thing to check

Pull the last five requests that touched both reps. For each one, ask who last spoke to the buyer, what they promised, and who accepted the next action. Two different answers tell you exactly where the reps need a clearer agreement. If it keeps happening across several accounts, revisit how you assign sales territories and account ownership before making the team attend another status meeting.
