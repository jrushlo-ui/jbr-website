How to Handle a Distributor Backorder Before the Customer Has to Call

By Josh Rushlo · JBR Insights · Sales Operations · November 25, 2026

The worst time for a buyer to learn about a backorder is when they count what came off the truck. The next worst time is after your last carrier pickup, when an option they might have accepted is already gone.

When a confirmed order comes up short, give one person the buyer call. Have them verify what can still ship, find out when the customer actually needs it, and set a time for the next update. You can make that call while the supplier is still checking its schedule.

It’s 8:10 Wednesday and 18 cases are short

Here’s a fictional order. A customer bought 48 cases of pallet covers for Thursday delivery. During picking, the warehouse finds water damage and can release only 30. The inventory screen still shows 48. The rep checks with the warehouse lead, who reserves the 30 usable cases for this order. The supplier acknowledges the shortage but has yet to confirm a replacement date.

Shipping confirms that today’s truck can get the 30 cases there Thursday if it has instructions by 11 a.m. Two deliveries would add $35 to the original freight charge. The sales manager authorizes the distributor to absorb the extra $35. Now the rep can explain the actual cost to the buyer before asking them to accept a split.

What the buyer needs to hear before the cutoff

We found damage while picking your pallet covers. We have 30 of the 48 cases ready and can deliver those Thursday. I’m waiting on a confirmed date for the other 18. If two deliveries work for you, we’ll cover the extra freight. How many cases do you need for Thursday’s run?

I’ll call by 10:30 this morning either way. That gives us time to keep the first truck if you want it.

Suppose the buyer needs 20 cases on Thursday and agrees to take all 30. The rep asks when the buyer will need the balance, records the split approval, and gives shipping the instructions and freight decision. The delivery paperwork shows the outstanding balance so receiving can reconcile the packing slip with the original order.

What if the supplier still has no date?

Keep the promised update. “The first 30 are booked for Thursday. The supplier is checking another branch for the remaining 18. I’ll update you Friday at 9 a.m.” Only mention the other branch if someone is actually checking it. Keep the order on one person’s follow-up list until the buyer receives the balance or agrees to a different resolution.

If the buyer needs all 48 together, check the transfer, alternate product or cancellation options your order terms allow. Confirm specifications, total cost and timing before asking the buyer to choose. If the first call goes unanswered, send the facts in writing and flag the cutoff internally. An unanswered voicemail gives you no agreement to change the shipment. Make sure the buyer’s eventual decision reaches the people doing the picking through the sales-to-operations handoff.
