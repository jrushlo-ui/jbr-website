Five Simple Ways to Use AI in a Small Business

By Josh Rushlo · JBR Insights · Everyday Business AI · November 25, 2026

If you’ve never used AI at work, start with something you can check in five minutes. Open a chat tool your company approves, paste a small piece of text, and ask for one useful thing back. No software integration or special vocabulary required. A good first task is something you know well enough to catch a bad answer.

1. Compare two supplier quotes

Say you run a small print shop and need the office cleaned four times next month. Here are two fictional offers. Company A wants $480 for four visits, cleaning supplies included. Company B wants $115 a visit and asks you to provide supplies. Both offer the same cleaning tasks. You could paste those three sentences into the chat and use this request.

Compare what I’d pay for four visits. Show the math, list what each price includes, and give me one question I should ask before choosing. Use only the details I gave you.

A useful answer would show $480 for A and $460 for B before supplies. The difference is $20. If the supplies you’d have to buy for B cost more than $20 for those four visits, A costs less overall. The next question for B is which supplies you’d need to provide and in what quantities. The two quotes give you no basis for guessing that cost.

2. Make a checklist from instructions you already trust

Give it the three things your receiving lead does when a delivery arrives. For example, “Count the boxes, compare the count with the packing slip, and tell Pat about any shortage before filing the slip.” Ask for a checklist a new hire can use, in that order. Check that Pat’s name and the timing survived the rewrite. That’s the detail someone learning the job needs.

3. Clean up a note to your staff

Start with the version you’d text a coworker. “Tuesday inventory 1 to 3 p.m. Front counter closed. Deliveries still come in side door.” Ask for a two-sentence staff notice. You should get something like “The front counter will be closed Tuesday from 1 to 3 p.m. for inventory. Please direct deliveries to the side door during that time.” Read it once before sending.

4. Sort a list without losing an item

Paste a supply list and ask it to group items by where they go. Envelopes and mailing labels belong in the mailroom. Tea and coffee go in the break room. Add “Keep the quantities exactly as written and put anything unclear under ‘Ask me.’” Compare the item count with your original before placing the order.

5. Turn repeat questions into a customer handout

Paste your approved answers about pickup hours, returns, and where to park. Ask for a one-page handout with the question a customer would actually ask above each answer. Check every hour and policy against the original. If you never gave it a holiday schedule, a good draft leaves that question open.

Try these with invented names and ordinary business details first. Leave employee records, customer contact information, and private account details out of the chat. If you’d like help picking a first task from your own workday, start with AI consulting for small business workflows.
