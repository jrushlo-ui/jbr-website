How to Find and Develop New Distribution Channels

By Josh Rushlo · JBR Insights · Channel Strategy · Published September 19, 2026

Finding a new distribution channel is not the same as building a list of distributors. A useful channel starts with a market problem: customers the current model does not reach well, geography that is expensive to cover directly, a buying process the company does not fit, or an adjacent market where another partner already has credibility.

Start With the Customer You Cannot Reach Efficiently

Before searching for partners, define the customer segment, geography, application, or buying behavior that creates the opportunity. Where do those customers buy today? Who already calls on them? What other products do they purchase at the same time? Those questions usually reveal better channel options than searching for “distributors” in a directory.

Map the Existing Route to Market

Look at how the product moves from the company to the end user today. Identify where coverage is strong, where it depends on a few relationships, and where prospects disappear because the current sales model is too expensive or too slow. A new channel should solve a specific coverage problem rather than simply add another layer.

Evaluate Partners for Fit

Reach matters, but attention matters too. A large distributor with hundreds of competing lines may create less opportunity than a focused partner whose customers, field team, and priorities align with the business. Evaluate customer overlap, geographic strength, complementary products, sales capability, reputation, service expectations, economics, and willingness to actively develop the line.

Define How the Channel Will Work Before Scaling It

Account ownership, pricing, lead handling, territory expectations, direct-sales interaction, training, support, and communication should not be invented after conflict appears. Build the operating rules early, test them with a manageable number of partners, and adjust based on what happens in the field.

Manage the Channel Like a Sales System

Signing the agreement is the beginning. Productive channels need opportunity reviews, joint planning, field communication, market feedback, partner support, and clear expectations. If nobody owns partner development after onboarding, the channel usually becomes a list of names rather than a source of growth.

The best new channel is not the one with the most logos. It is the one that reaches customers the current sales model cannot serve efficiently—and has a system behind it.
