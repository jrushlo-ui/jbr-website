How to Onboard a New Distributor in the First 90 Days

By Josh Rushlo · JBR Insights · Distributor Channels · November 25, 2026

The distributor’s opening order just shipped. A month from now, a counter rep is going to get a call from a customer who asks, “Will this fit what I already have?” Who at the branch can answer? If everybody has to hunt down the manufacturer’s rep, there’s still work to do on this launch.

For a line that customers buy regularly, I’d give the first 90 days a specific finish line. Can the distributor help a customer choose the right product, fulfill the order, and win the next one? The opening stock order gives the partner something to sell. The customer’s second purchase tells you considerably more.

Before the first customer call

Take a fictional launch of packaging tape at a two-branch industrial distributor. The manufacturer has twelve grades. The distributor starts with two that fit the cartons and dispensers its customers already use. The branch manager names a rep at each location, and each rep brings three accounts that buy packaging supplies. Now six actual buyers give the training session a purpose.

In the first 30 days, have one of those reps work through an order from the customer’s side of the counter. Can they find the correct item in the system, explain the difference between the two grades, quote a case, and see stock at their branch? The manufacturer brings sample rolls and answers application questions. The branch manager fixes an item listed by the roll when it’s sold by the case. Catching that before six customers get the wrong price is a good first-month result.

Put the product in a buyer’s hands

By day 60, the two reps should be able to tell you what happened at those six accounts. In our example, two customers agreed to run a sample on their packing line. One reported that the tape lifted after the boxes sat overnight. The rep gets the carton type and storage conditions back to the manufacturer. The other customer ordered four cases for a single production line.

That four-case order deserves a question while the buyer still has the trial fresh in mind. “How long will four cases last on this line, and who replenishes the shelf?” The buyer expects three weeks. The distributor schedules a call with the person who orders consumables. The manufacturer helps troubleshoot the first account’s application.

Use day 90 to make a stocking decision

Suppose the four-case customer reorders on schedule and the other trial is still unresolved. I’d keep the proven grade available, work through the failed trial, and hold off on adding ten more grades. Record the repeat purchase against the end customer. A second PO from the distributor to fill another branch answers a different question.

For products with a longer replacement cycle, agree on evidence you can reasonably expect in 90 days, such as a completed installation and the buyer’s scheduled replenishment review. Use the distributor performance scorecard to carry those commitments into the next quarter. If launches keep stopping at the opening order, bring one to a channel strategy conversation.

Check how much product actually sold through after the opening order.
