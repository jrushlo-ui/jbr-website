How to Run a Quote Win-Loss Review in Wholesale Distribution

By Josh Rushlo · JBR Insights · Sales Operations · November 25, 2026

You ask why the branch lost a quote. The rep says price. The buyer says, “We needed the valves Friday. Yours were coming Monday.” Cut the price on the next quote and you could lose the same order for less money.

A quote win-loss review gives a sales leader a way to find out which buying decisions the team can learn from. Pick a period, pull the quotes that reached an outcome during it, and read the buyer’s explanation wherever you have one.

Decide what counts as a buying decision

Use one record per buying decision. A price revision on Tuesday and a freight revision on Wednesday can belong to the same quote. Count that decision once. If the customer split an award, track the share you won separately and agree how split awards will appear in your reporting.

Close a quote as won when you have an accepted order. Mark a competitive loss when you know another supplier got the business. Mark no decision when the buyer confirms the purchase was canceled or shelved and nobody got the order. A quiet inbox tells you very little about which of those happened. Keep unresolved quotes out of these counts and take them to the weekly pipeline review.

One branch, two different win rates

In a fictional month, a branch resolves 30 quotes. It wins 15 orders, loses nine to another supplier, and confirms that six projects were shelved. Another five quotes are still open. The sales manager writes down both numbers the leadership team has been calling “win rate.”

  • Share of competitive decisions won. The branch won 15 of the 24 purchases made, or 62.5%.
  • Share of all resolved quotes won. It won 15 of 30 resolved decisions, or 50%. This view includes time spent quoting work buyers eventually shelved.

Of the nine competitive losses, three buyers named delivery timing, two named a specification the competing product met, and one named price. The other three gave no reason. That leaves one documented price loss. It also gives the manager three delivery losses worth opening with the shipping lead.

What would you change on the next quote?

Pull one win and one loss from comparable orders. Ask the reps to show you the buyer’s words, which requirement decided the purchase, and when your team first learned about it. When a rep offers an interpretation, keep it in the notes as a theory until someone can check it. A short call can start with, “When you compared the offers, what ended up deciding it for you?”

For the valve quote, I’d look for the customer’s required arrival date on the original request. If the rep learned it only after losing, try asking for a required-on-site date at intake. For the next ten time-sensitive quotes, have the manager check whether that date reached the person confirming availability. That gives the branch one change to test and a concrete question for its next sales process review.
